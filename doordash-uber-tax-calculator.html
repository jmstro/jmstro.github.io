Tips for DoorDash, Uber & Lyft

  • Miles count from your first trip: miles driven to a pickup, between orders and while working count. Commuting from home to a regular workplace doesn't, but most app drivers have no regular workplace.
  • Mileage rate covers the car: if you use the standard mileage rate, don't also deduct gas, repairs, insurance or depreciation.
  • Keep a log: the IRS wants date, miles and purpose. App mileage reports often miss the miles between orders.

Questions

Do I owe tax if my 1099 is under $600?

Yes. All self-employment profit is taxable, and self-employment tax applies once net earnings reach $400, whether or not you get a 1099.

Should I use the mileage rate or actual car expenses?

Most full-time drivers come out ahead with the standard mileage rate, and it's far less record-keeping. If you choose actual expenses, you generally have to stick with that method for that car.

Is this calculator accurate?

It follows IRS brackets, standard deductions and the Social Security wage base for 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32) and 2027 (Rev. Proc. 2026-35), and it simplifies a few things: no credits, no itemized deductions, no additional 0.9% Medicare tax, and a simplified QBI deduction. Treat it as a planning estimate, not tax advice.
